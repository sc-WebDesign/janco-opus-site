## Why only four, and why they are all close-ups

The appliance-repair research (`research/_niche-research/appliance-repair.md`) found this is the
**one trade studied where photography is not the competitive norm**: the two leading UK sites run on
appliance icons and manufacturer logos, one with essentially no photographs at all. So four good
photographs is already ahead of the field here rather than behind it.

They are all close-ups by design. **Stock libraries are dominated by American interiors**: searches
for laundry rooms and fitted kitchens returned US homes with US sockets, US doors and visible US
brand marks, none of which can be passed off as an Essex house. Close-ups of appliances, tools and
cable are geography-neutral and true. The mains cable in particular shows **brown, blue and
green-yellow cores**, which is the correct UK convention.

## Selection method

Every file was verified as a real JPEG by its magic bytes (`ffd8ff`) and then **opened and looked at
before use**. Twelve candidates were rejected across two sessions. Recorded so they are not tried
again:

- `8774444` a commercial laundrette machine, not a domestic appliance
- `9462228` a pastel microwave being cleaned, not repaired
- `5846253` a dark metalworking bench, wrong trade
- `909256` a pegboard with novelty paper hearts numbered one to ten stuck on the tools
- `6349399` a sterile catalogue shot on white with a tool brand repeated five times
- `17158638` an American laundry room
- `10827395` an American kitchen carrying a visible appliance brand mark
- `9242911` robotics with legible "Robot Moves on Initialization" warning labels
- `GzLxFhvgzkw` a dim green-cast laundry room
- `oAPv2AoMmCU` a car workshop with a vehicle on a two-post lift, wrong trade entirely
- plus two Unsplash downloads that returned zero bytes

**No manufacturer brand logos appear anywhere**, deliberately. The research found competitors lean
on them heavily, and they imply an authorised-repairer relationship that has not been verified.
